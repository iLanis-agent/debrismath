Boundary: arithmetic only Debris Math counts debris with multiplication, division and rounding-up: the cubic yards a pile takes, the dumpsters it fills, the tons it weighs, and the bill with overage. It applies the counting as written, so it has not seen your pile and does not know what your material really weighs, what your hauler accepts, your permits or any hazardous material rules, and it gives no verdict that a load is allowed or fits a weight limit. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: a pile 20 ft by 10 ft by 3 ft, 20 cu yd dumpsters, 400 lb a cubic yard, $400 a dumpster with 2 tons included and $75 a ton over.

The volume truth

The pile of 20 ft by 10 ft by 3 ft is 22.22 cu yd.

The loads and weight truths

The 22 cu yd in dumpsters of 20 cu yd takes 2 dumpsters, leaving 18 cu yd of room in the last. The 22 cu yd at 400 lb a cubic yard weigh 8800 lb, or 4.4 tons.

The cost truth

The 2 dumpsters at $400 for 4.4 tons, with 2 tons included each, run over by 0.4 tons and cost $830.00.
